# A wall in a louvre window

A louvre window, or jalousie, has glass blades held at each end in clips, which a handle turns
together; closed, each blade overlaps the one below, and the lowest closes onto a weatherstrip on
the sill (relayed, in the survey of window types,
[`research/window-types.md`](research/window-types.md), and in Breezway's
[Altair pocket guide](https://www.breezway.com.au/downloads/australia/Pocket_Guide.pdf), whose
heights are "galleries + weatherstrip"). No kit for an air conditioner's hose or a fan was found
for one (relayed, in the survey).

The wall takes the place of the lowest blades (C344, C345):

- **It stands on the sill**, where the lowest blade's weatherstrip is.
- **Its sides are pressed against the clips** of the blades taken out, closed as the handle leaves
  them, clear of the channels the clips turn in; their printed TPU gaskets' squeeze holds it, as in
  a frame with no track ([`frame.md`](frame.md)).
- **Its top edge is under the lowest blade left**: closed by the handle, that blade comes down onto
  the wall's top gasket, as it would onto the window's own weatherstrip.

The tool sets these edges when you choose the window type; each can be changed below it.

## The setting

Under **The window**, choose **Window type: A louvre window** (the command: `--window louvre`; the
library and the MCP server: `edges.window: "louvre"`), and the printed TPU seal. Nothing else is
asked (C345).

## Measure your window

1. Decide how many blades come out: as many as the wall needs for what passes through it. With
   152 mm clips the blades are 140 mm apart, with 102 mm clips 90 mm (verified: the step between
   rows of Breezway's height chart), so four blades out leave about 560 mm, or 360 mm.
2. Take those blades out of their clips, the lowest first. Most clips let a blade slide out
   sideways once it is lifted past the clip's tab (inferred); a window whose blades are held by a
   retention system needs its maker's instructions. Keep the blades flat and safe: they are glass.
3. Close the louvres with the handle, and measure the opening: its **width** between the clips of
   the blades taken out, at their inner faces, and its **height** from the sill to the lowest blade
   left.

## Putting it in

1. Put the wall together, its gaskets in their grooves, and stand it on the sill between the clips,
   pushed in from the room side.
2. Close the louvres with the handle, so the lowest blade comes down onto the wall's top gasket.
3. Keep the louvres closed while the wall is in: the clips at its sides turn with the handle, and
   opening them would push on the wall's sides (C346). Nothing printed holds the handle.

The plan says the same: which blades come out, and to keep the louvres closed.

## Hardware in the way

The handle's gearbox sits on one jamb, often low (inferred). Where it is in the way of the wall's
side edge, leave a notch for it ([`frame.md`](frame.md)): the notches are offered for a louvre
window's side edges, as for a frame's (C347).

## Safety

As for any wall held by its gaskets: it is not a barrier against falling, nor a locked window, and
it comes out before a storm. With the blades out, a child can reach the opening once the wall is
taken out: put the blades back when it is.
