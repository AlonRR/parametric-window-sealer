# A wall in a roof window

A top-hung roof window opens outward at the bottom, to about 45°, in roofs pitched 15 to 55° (relayed,
in the survey of window types, [`research/window-types.md`](research/window-types.md)). Opened, it
leaves its frame clear from inside, as a casement opened outward does: the wall goes in from the
room as in a frame with no track ([`frame.md`](frame.md)), held by its printed TPU gaskets'
squeeze. On a slope, though, part of the wall's weight pulls it out of the frame into the room, and
only that squeeze holds it. The tool says how much, and how to test that it holds.

An awning opens outward too, but stands upright in a wall: choose a frame with no track for it.

## The setting

Under **The window**, choose **Window type: A roof window opened out** (the command:
`--window roof`; the library and the MCP server: `edges.window: "roof"`), and give the **roof
pitch**, in degrees from level, 15 to 90 (`--roof-pitch`, `roof.pitchDeg`; 35 to start with, the
middle of what a top-hung window is made for) (C323, C324). Every edge becomes a frame edge, with
the printed TPU seal; the parts are those of a frame with no track. Measure the opening inside the
frame, square to it.

## What holds it

The share of the wall's weight across the window, the cosine of the pitch, pulls it out of the
frame into the room: 82% at 35°, 50% at 60°, none at 90°. The rest pulls it down the slope onto
the frame's lower face, which carries it. How much grip the gaskets give is not measured yet, so
the tool does not guess it, nor the wall's weight (C325). Instead, the plan says, for your pitch:

1. **Weigh the parts** before you fit them, all together on a kitchen or bathroom scale.
2. **Fit the wall**, pushed in square from the room side.
3. **Test it:** push the middle of its room face with a scale held flat, up to the number of times
   its weight the plan gives, twice the share that pulls it out: 1.6 times at 35° (C326). The
   gaskets' grip holds the same either way, so pushing tests what gravity pulls (inferred). It must
   not move.
4. **Until it has passed, and wherever someone sits or sleeps below it,** hold it up with a strap
   across its room face, fixed to the frame or the lining round it.

With a hose port, hang the hose from a hook above the window, so its weight does not pull on the
wall.

## Rain

Rain on the wall runs down its slope to its bottom edge, and the frame's lower face slopes down to
the wall too, so water gathers against the bottom gasket (inferred). The sash, opened out over the
wall, shelters it from rain falling straight down; close the window when rain is blown under it
(C327). With rain on (the starting value), the strips' weep holes still drain the joints down the
slope.
