Parametric window sealer

Printable panels that close off the part of a window left open around an air conditioner, a fan or a hose.

Measuring guide: what to measure, with pictures

Each field here fills the form below as you type. The sizes in the drawings are examples; use a tape measure for the window and calipers for printed parts.

1. The opening

Close the window as far as it goes against what is in it, then measure the gap left open, inside the frame, at its narrowest. Do not count the track's depth here.

2. The track

Measure the track the window slides in: the width between its walls, and how deep it is from its lip to its floor. The depth goes to every edge that sits in a track.

3. What passes through the wall

Nothing passes through a plain wall. If something does, choose it under What is in the window?

Measure across the outside of the hose end that goes into the port, not the hose itself.

Measure the outside of what passes through the wall, across and up, seen from the room.

Measure the opening left open around the unit, in step 1; the unit's own size is not needed.

4. A printed strip and piece

Print one joint strip and one piece from the download. With calipers, measure the strip's slot across, between its flanges and clear of the ribs inside them, and the piece's thickness. The guide works out the fit that gives the joint the play it is meant to have.

5. The grip

In the joint's advanced settings below, tick Add a test coupon to the download, and print the coupon as the strips will be printed. Push its tab into each of the five short strips, from the side without notches. The one that takes the tab with a firm push and holds it has the grip to use; the plan says each one's grip, and the notches say which is which.

The window

Sets what each edge of the wall does. Change any edge below if your window differs. Each edge gets an edge strip: in a track it fills the track; against the sash or on a sill it gives a flat face. Where the two sashes overlap, a gap runs between them from outside to the room. Measure it front to back where a filler would go, and one is printed to close it, as long as the edge the sash presses on, with the seal on both faces. 0 for none.

The wall is not a lock. A sash stop is a printed strut that lies in the track the sash runs in, from its edge to the frame, so the sash cannot be opened further from outside; it lifts out by hand from inside, so the window still opens as an escape. Measure that gap along the track; 0 for none.

Seal

Self-adhesive closed-cell foam weather-seal tape: felt and open-cell foam let air through. It goes in a groove along each edge strip's outer face, where the track, the sash or the sill presses on it.

Gaskets printed in TPU, in place of tape of this size: each sits in a groove with two fins leaning in, which fold flat into it as the wall goes in, until the strip's face stops them. TPU 85A springs back better than 95A, which is easier to print. Print the gaskets in TPU and the rest as usual.

The panel

Each plain printed piece is hollow inside the panel thickness: two 1.2 mm skins round an air gap, which roughly halves the heat lost through the wall. It needs panels at least 6.4 mm thick, and the track must take them.

PLA softens in a summer window and beside a hose's exhaust; PETG in a light colour holds up, and ASA in sun. The plan warns where the wall would get near what the material takes.

Every strip gets weep holes in one flange, so water that gets into its slots runs out again: fit that flange outside. Edge strips with no seal get a groove underneath that stops water creeping under them.

                                Before you fit it

                                • The wall is not a fall barrier: it will not stop a child, or anyone, falling out of the window.
                                • Do not block a bedroom’s only escape window unless the wall comes out in seconds, by hand, with no tool.
                                • On a ground floor, or wherever the window can be reached from outside, add a metal window lock: neither the wall nor its sash stop is a lock.
                                • In winter, check the wall and the window for condensation, and air the room if they run wet.
                                • PLA softens in a sunny window: print the wall in PETG, or in ASA where it gets sun.
                                • Printed plastics burn: keep the wall away from heaters and flames, and do not trap a power cord in it.
                                • Put the wall together whole, away from the window, then set it in the window in one piece.

                                This project claims no energy saving, and the wall is not airtight, weatherproof, secure, child-safe or flame retardant; it has no wind, rain or UV rating.
